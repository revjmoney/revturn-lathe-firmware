R9 INLINE RAIL MOTOR MOUNT — Ender 3 Pro Z coupler
================================================

Print:
  1 x inline_motor_mount.stl
  2 x rail_clamp_cap_PRINT_2.stl

These STLs already sit on the print bed:
- Main bracket: motor face down. Use build-plate supports.
- Caps: flat outer top down. No supports.
- Saved Ender profile was checked at 0.3 mm layers, 5 perimeters, 35% infill.
  The STL does not contain these settings: set them in your slicer.
- Slice estimates: main 2h 47m 37s, each cap 21m 37s.
  Total for all three pieces: about 3h 31m.
- Largest print footprint: 54.3 x 67 mm, height 59.5 mm.
- Deburr/clean supports and test the rail seats before tightening.
  The rail seat is 10.0322 mm for the R9 model's 9.9822 mm rails.
  Tightening the caps closes the 0.4 mm split gap.

Hardware:
- Your Ender 3 Pro Z coupler, 5 mm motor bore / 8 mm screw bore.
  Axial layout assumes 25 mm length. Clearance model is 20 mm OD;
  it also passes with a 24 mm OD envelope.
- Four M3 x 20 mm cap screws, four M3 nuts (5.5 mm across flats,
  up to 2.4 mm thick) and thin M3 washers. Hex pockets are 5.7 mm AF.
  Check actual screw protrusion and hardware sizes during assembly.
- Four M3 motor screws. With the 6.35 mm plate, M3 x 10 provides
  about 3.65 mm motor-thread engagement; verify the motor's permissible
  thread depth before choosing screw length.
- NEMA 17 pattern: 31 mm centres, 3.4 mm clearance holes,
  23.5 mm pilot opening. Motor body envelope 42.3 mm square.
- Actual donor motor shaft projection is unmeasured: source uses 24 mm.

Fit:
1. Establish the outside face of the pulley-end tower as the datum.
   Keep both rails extending about 30 mm beyond this face.
   The existing 140 mm projection must be shortened before fitting:
   it intersects the inline motor body. Measure on the assembled machine
   before making the cut; do not trim into the tower's rail support.
2. Retain the R9 screw bearings and the fixed-end collars.
   The pulley-end tower is the floating bearing end in this R9 source.
   The coupling/motor must not become the screw's thrust bearing.
3. Place the bracket's lower clamp halves under the rail extensions,
   fit the two identical caps above and insert the four captive nuts.
   Turn one cap 180 degrees in the horizontal plane so the small inner
   leadscrew reliefs both face towards the screw.
   Start the screws loosely; both rail seats must contact evenly.
4. Motor faces the slide, shaft inward along the screw (-X).
   Slide the entire bracket along the rail ends to set axial fit, then
   tighten caps evenly. No original tower mounting holes are required
   for this rail-clamped direct-drive revision.
5. Nominal clamp grip: 4–24 mm beyond tower. Permitted positions:
   1–21 mm through 10–30 mm beyond tower, keeping the whole 20 mm grip.
   This gives 9 mm total physical axial adjustment.
6. At the nominal position the screw tip projects 38.5 mm beyond the
   tower, with 12 mm coupling engagement on each shaft and 1 mm
   between shaft ends. At the adjustment extremes the corresponding
   screw projection is 35.5–44.5 mm. These dimensions assume the
   25 mm coupler and 24 mm motor shaft; adjust the screw projection
   and bracket position together, without shifting the screw's bearing
   retention or reducing travel.
7. Coupling setscrews are accessible from above; orient them to suit
   your tool. Verify shaft bore fit, engagement and true running by
   turning the screw manually before powering the motor.

Rounded structure:
- Four motor plate corner gussets.
- 2 mm outer plate/cap corner radii.
- 0.6 mm rounds on selected exposed edges.
- Functional rail seats, split faces, bolt bores and nut pockets retained.

Direct-drive setup:
- Assumed screw lead: 4 mm per revolution (confirm by measurement).
- A 200-full-step/revolution motor gives 0.02 mm slide motion per
  full step, corresponding to 0.04 mm change in turned diameter.
- Example with 16 microsteps: 800 commanded steps/mm. Confirm the
  donor motor's actual step angle and driver microstep setting.
- Motor torque, printed clamp stiffness and cutting load have not been
  physically verified. The original belt reduction had more screw torque.
  Start with low acceleration and light cuts, and verify no lost position.

Files:

- inline_mount_assembly.FCStd / .step include all three printed pieces plus
