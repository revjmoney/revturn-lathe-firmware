R9 ONE-PIECE INLINE MOTOR MOUNT — SOLID HALF SEATS + SIDE TIE
===========================================================

Print ONE:
inline_motor_mount_SOLID_HALF_SIDE_TIE_PRINT.stl

Requested revision:
- Exactly 1.250" between outside pulley-end tower face and the INSIDE
  face of the motor mounting plate.
- Two solid lower half-round rail seats, integral with the bracket.
  Open above the rail centreline; no separate caps or rail-clamp screws.
- Four original R9 M4 side holes, unchanged in position.
- 0.250" side flange with a continuous web joining the positive rail seat.
- 0.250" motor plate, four corner gussets and rounded exposed edges.
- Motor body depth updated to your measured approximately 1.750".

Fit dimensions, in inches:
- Original model rail diameter: 0.393"; centre spacing: 1.496".
- Half-seat diameter: about 0.397" (0.004" diametral clearance).
- Rail contact length: 0.787".
- Seats span 0.157" to 0.945" beyond tower face.
- Keep both rail projections about 1.181" beyond tower face:
  shorten the long rail to match the existing short rail.
- Motor plate inside face: 1.250" beyond tower.
- Motor front mounting face: 1.500" beyond tower.
- Coupler model length: 0.984", outer envelope diameter: 0.787".
- Central relief diameter: 0.945", providing 0.079" radial clearance
  to the modeled coupler through the rail-seat area.
- Coupler starts 0.043" beyond tower face.
- Screw tip projects approximately 0.516" beyond tower.
- Each shaft engages approximately 0.472" into the coupler.
- Gap between shaft ends: about 0.039".

These axial fit figures assume a motor shaft projecting 0.945" and a
0.984"-long coupler. The donor motor's exposed shaft length is still
unmeasured. Check that dimension and actual coupler bore depths before
printing or setting the screw length. For a different shaft/coupler,
edit parameters.json and rebuild while retaining the 1.250" plate gap.

Attachment:
- Four original side M4 holes retain the mount. Their rectangular
  pattern is about 0.356" along X by 0.682" vertically.
  The pattern centre is 0.375" inboard of the tower's outside face,
  centred vertically on the original screw axis.
- Side flange clearance holes are approximately 0.177" diameter.
  Metric hardware is retained because it matches the existing R9 holes.
- Nominal four M4 x 16 mm screws with thin flat washers suit the
  0.250" flange and modeled 11 mm original thread depth. Verify the
  actual tower threads and avoid bottoming screws.
- Four M3 motor screws retain the original 31 mm NEMA 17 pattern.
  M3 x 10 mm gives about 0.144" motor-thread engagement through this
  plate, before washers; verify the motor's allowable thread depth.
- With the four side screws installed, this bracket has a FIXED axial
  position. The previous sliding rail-clamp adjustment is superseded.
- Place the lower half seats beneath the projecting rails and seat
  the flange against the original +Y tower face. Start all four side
  screws loosely, check smooth screw rotation and coupling alignment,
  then tighten evenly.
- Preserve the original fixed-end bearings and collars. The coupler
  and motor do not replace the screw's thrust retention.
- Coupling setscrews remain accessible from above.

Printing:
- Supplied PRINT STL is already oriented with motor face down.
- Use build-plate supports; the rail seats and side flange overhang.
- Verification uses the saved Ender profile, approximately 0.012" layers,
  five perimeters and 35% infill. Apply those settings in your slicer.
- Remove supports and deburr all locating faces/holes before fitting.
- See slice-verification.json for the final time/material estimate.

Files:

- *_assembly.stl / main STEP: original R9 assembly coordinates.
- Main FCStd: only the printable bracket.
- inline_side_tie_assembly.FCStd / .step: bracket plus reference saddle,

- verification.json: geometry, full travel, hole alignment, screw access,
  coupling clearance, connected solid and mesh checks.
- solid-half-mount-preview.png: assembly and inch-dimensioned layout.

This is a geometric fit-checked prototype. Actual hardware fit, printed
stiffness, torque, cutting load and durability remain untested.
